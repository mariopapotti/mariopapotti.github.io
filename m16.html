M16 – Eagle Nebula

Messier 16 • NGC 6611 • Serpens

About the Object

La Nebulosa Aquila, nota anche come Nebulosa Regina delle Stelle, fu scoperta da Philippe Loys de Chéseaux nel 1746. In seguito fu osservata da Charles Messier che la catalogò come Messier 16 o M16. È un giovane ammasso aperto di circa 500 stelle nella costellazione del Serpente, a circa 5700 anni luce dalla Terra.

Questa nebulosa è famosa per contenere al proprio interno alcune strutture ultradense come i Pilastri della Creazione, immortalati dal telescopio Hubble nel 1995, e la Guglia Stellare o Fata della Nebulosa Aquila, una fantastica colonna di gas e polveri, sede di formazione stellare che si innalza nella regione.

Queste strutture sono state create dall'azione del vento stellare delle stelle giganti dell'ammasso aperto centrale.

How to Find It

The constellations of Ophiuchus and Serpens are closely connected in the summer sky. Ophiuchus divides Serpens into two separate parts: Serpens Caput (the Head) to the west and Serpens Cauda (the Tail) to the east.

M16 is located just west of Serpens Cauda. It can be found starting from the star γ Scuti (Gamma Scuti) and moving approximately 3° toward WSW.

The best observing season is from June to October, when the Eagle Nebula reaches its highest position in the evening sky.

About This Image

Like many astrophotographers, I have always been fascinated by the Pillars of Creation.

I first photographed M16 in 2024 using a 150/600 Newtonian telescope. Although the image was satisfying, the field of view was too wide to properly highlight the intricate structures inside the nebula.

Thanks to my new Tecnosky RC8 Ritchey-Chrétien telescope I was finally able to capture the Eagle Nebula with a much tighter framing, revealing the extraordinary details of this remarkable star-forming region.

Processing

The image was processed entirely in PixInsight using my personal RGB workflow, including WBPP calibration, SPCC colour calibration, noise reduction, stretching, colour refinement and final contrast enhancement.

Behind the Image

The greatest challenge during processing was preserving the delicate oxygen emission without resorting to false-colour palettes.

My goal was to keep the image as natural as possible while still enhancing the subtle blue tones surrounding the Pillars of Creation.

The final appearance was achieved using Generalized Hyperbolic Stretch (GHS) followed by careful colour adjustments with CurvesTransformation.
